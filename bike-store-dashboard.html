Revenue over time

Monthly, Jan 2016 – Dec 2018

Monthly net revenue

Bars are monthly revenue; the line is a 3-month moving average. The hatched region marks the sparse window described in the ledger.

Reading it: across the 28 dense months, revenue climbs steadily — 2016 averaged $202K/month, 2017 averaged $287K/month, and Jan–Apr 2018 averaged $441K/month. April 2018 ($817,922) is the largest month in the file by a wide margin and lands immediately before the data thins out, so it should be treated as a boundary artifact rather than a demand spike until it can be confirmed against source systems.

Discount behaviour

Pricing analysis

Where the $889,872 goes

Only four discount rates appear anywhere in the data: 5%, 7%, 10% and 20%. Bars show revenue booked at each rate; the marked portion is the discount given away.

The 20% rate is the whole story. It covers 25.5% of order lines but produces 47.2% of total discount given — $419,791. Cutting that rate to 10% on the same lines would have returned roughly $210K to the top line.

Discount depth by price tier

Average discount rate applied, grouped by product list price.

The discount is flat. A $200 children's bike and a $6,000 road bike both get an average of about 10.5% off. Average depth ranges only from 10.41% to 10.63% across all four price tiers, and from 10.21% to 11.13% across all seven categories. Discounts here are not responding to price, margin or category — they look assigned rather than decided.

What sells

Category, brand, product

Revenue by category

Net revenue, all seven categories.

Volume and value disagree. Cruisers sell the most units (2,063) but rank third in revenue. Road Bikes sell 559 units — a quarter of the cruiser volume — and out-earn them by $670K.

Revenue by brand

Net revenue. Nine brands carry sales; one has none.

Concentration risk: Trek alone accounts for 59.9% of revenue — Electra, the next brand, holds 15.7%. A supply disruption or margin change with a single vendor would move the whole business.

Stores and staff

Three locations, six selling staff

Revenue by store

Net revenue by location (NY, CA, TX).

Baldwin Bikes (NY) is 67.8% of the business on 67.7% of orders. Rowlett (TX) turns over $867K — one sixth of Baldwin — despite drawing from a comparable metro. Two reps at Baldwin — Marcelene Boyer and Venita Daniel — personally account for $5.22M, the entire store. Worth checking headcount before reading Rowlett as a demand problem.

Method Schema and 4,722 order lines migrated from MySQL to SQLite with a Python script that maps column types and preserves keys. Revenue is computed per order line as quantity × list_price × (1 − discount) and rolled up with pandas. Joins span orders, order_items, products, brands, categories, stores, staffs and customers. Charts on this page are hand-built SVG with no charting library.
